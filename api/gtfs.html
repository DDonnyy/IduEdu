GTFS public transport¶

IduEdu can build a static public-transport iduedu.UrbanGraph from a local GTFS Schedule feed. The source may be a directory containing *.txt tables, a ZIP archive, an already-read iduedu.gtfs.GTFSFeed, or a list of those merged into one feed (see Merging several feeds). The GTFS public transport notebook walks through every step below on small synthetic feeds.

Building a graph¶

from iduedu import get_gtfs_public_transport_graph

public_transport = get_gtfs_public_transport_graph(
    "feed.zip",
    service_date="2026-08-04",
    start_time="07:00:00",
    end_time="10:00:00",
)

service_date applies calendar.txt and calendar_dates.txt. It accepts a Python date or datetime, YYYY-MM-DD, or YYYYMMDD. start_time is inclusive and end_time is exclusive; both accept GTFS time strings, Python time values, or seconds. An end time earlier than the start time selects an overnight window.

Without service_date the graph holds every route pattern that runs on at least one day, and its waiting times are those of the average day: each distinct service day is computed on its own trips, and the waits are averaged with every calendar day weighing the same, over the days the pattern runs. The days are the feed’s own calendar (at most 366 days from its start) or service_period=(first, last), for example one reference week. A gap is only ever a gap between departures of the same day: before October 2026 the trips of all days were put on one clock, and a weekday departure at 08:00 next to a Saturday one at 08:10 gave a 10-minute “gap” no passenger saw – waits came out at about half of any real day’s. start_time/end_time apply within each day, e.g. start_time="06:00", end_time="22:00" for daytime service.

# the average day of one reference week, daytime service only
average_week = get_gtfs_public_transport_graph(
    "feed.zip",
    service_period=("2026-10-12", "2026-10-18"),
    start_time="06:00",
    end_time="22:00",
)

service_dates=[...] lists the days explicitly instead, for example a reference week plus the own weeks of merged sources that were not in service then. Every day still holds only its own trips, so days of different years do not mix. service_date, service_period and service_dates cannot be combined. A feed without calendar.txt/calendar_dates.txt is taken as a single day.

Required and optional tables¶

The builder requires agency.txt, stops.txt, routes.txt, trips.txt, and stop_times.txt. At least one of calendar.txt and calendar_dates.txt must also be present.

The builder also reads frequencies.txt, shapes.txt, pathways.txt, levels.txt, and feed_info.txt when available. transfers.txt and exact time-dependent transfer routing are not implemented yet.

Base route types are represented as tram (0), subway (1), train (2), bus (3), ferry (4), cable_tram (5), aerial_lift (6), funicular (7), trolleybus (11), and monorail (12). Google’s extended route types are mapped by explicit ranges: 100-117 train, 200-209 coach (kept apart from city buses), 400-404 and 406-499 subway, 405 monorail, 700-716 bus, 800-899 trolleybus, 900-906 tram, 1000 and 1200 ferry, 1300 aerial_lift, 1400 funicular, and 1500 taxi. Any other code, including the miscellaneous 300, 500, 600, 1600 and 1700 ranges, remains public_transport rather than a guess from neighbouring codes.

Static time model¶

Trips with the same route, direction, shape, stop order, and pickup/drop-off rules form a route pattern. For each pattern segment, time_min is the median scheduled time from departure at one stop to arrival at the next.

Boarding edges represent expected waiting rather than vehicle motion:

  • scheduled service uses half the mean gap between consecutive departures of the line at the stop on the same day, averaged over days as described above. A line is the route leaving the stop for the same next stop: all its patterns count – branches on their shared section, short workings, expresses – so the wait is that of the line a rider waits for, not of one variant of it. The next stop, not direction_id, decides the direction;

  • frequencies.txt service uses headway_secs / 120 and duration-weighted averaging when frequency periods overlap the selected window;

  • a pattern stop with only one scheduled departure has no estimable headway, so its boarding edge is omitted by default. Set single_departure_wait_min to retain it with an explicit fallback.

Boarding and alighting edges always have length_meter = 0. Alighting also has time_min = 0. GTFS waiting times come from the feed and do not use iduedu.TransportRegistry defaults.

Geometry and distance¶

When shapes.txt supplies usable route geometry, segment geometry is cut from the matching shape and length_meter is its measured length. Otherwise the builder creates a straight line between consecutive route-stop points and assigns sqrt(2) * straight_line_length as the estimated travel distance. The square-root-of-two correction applies only to vehicle movement edges such as bus, tram, or subway; it never applies to boarding or alighting.

The graph CRS must be projected in metres. If crs is omitted, IduEdu estimates a local UTM CRS from the feed stops.

Merging several feeds¶

A city is often published as several feeds, and every feed numbers its own routes, trips, and stops. Pass a list of sources to merge them before the graph is built:

public_transport = get_gtfs_public_transport_graph(
    ["subway.zip", "bus.zip"],
    service_date="2026-08-04",
    start_time="07:00:00",
    end_time="10:00:00",
)

A list is merged with iduedu.merge_gtfs_feeds(), which qualifies every identifier by its source, so route 1 of one feed and route 1 of another stay distinct. Call it directly to choose the prefixes or to fuse stops that different agencies publish for the same place:

from iduedu import get_gtfs_public_transport_graph, merge_gtfs_feeds

feed = merge_gtfs_feeds(["subway.zip", "bus.zip"], merge_stops_within=20)
public_transport = get_gtfs_public_transport_graph(feed, service_date="2026-08-04")

Stops are kept distinct by default: fusing them changes the network topology, and an inferred transfer would look like a published one. Distances of about 15-25 m suit feeds from different agencies.

Feeds gathered from several catalogues often overlap: two versions of one feed, a national feed and a city feed of the same operator, a mirror and its original. deduplicate=True counts that service once. Platforms of different sources closer than duplicate_platform_tolerance (0.5 m by default – the same coordinate republished, while distinct platforms of one feed already occur 1-3 m apart) become one platform, and a trip that another source already runs through the same platforms at the same times is dropped. Platforms of one feed are never fused with each other, nor stations, entrances or boarding areas:

feed = merge_gtfs_feeds(["city.zip", "national.zip"], deduplicate=True)

iduedu.deduplicate_across_feeds() applies the same rules to tables merged elsewhere and returns how many platforms and trips it removed.

Stations, pathways, and intermodal joining¶

pathways.txt creates station-internal pathway edges. Explicit traversal_time is converted from seconds to minutes; otherwise time is estimated from pathway length and walk_speed_m_per_min. Station entrances, nodes, boarding areas, and platforms are retained when referenced by the served stops or pathways.

A stop that names a parent_station is linked to it with a zero-cost, bidirectional station_link edge. Only stops already present in the graph are linked. Without these edges, a station described down to boarding areas would leave its platforms in a separate component that keep_largest_subgraph removes.

To join GTFS public transport to an OSM walking graph, build the walk layer first and use the same CRS:

from iduedu import get_gtfs_public_transport_graph, get_walk_graph, join_pt_walk_graph

walk = get_walk_graph(osm_id=1114252)
public_transport = get_gtfs_public_transport_graph(
    "feed.zip",
    service_date="2026-08-04",
    start_time="07:00:00",
    end_time="10:00:00",
    crs=walk.crs,
)
intermodal = join_pt_walk_graph(public_transport, walk)

API reference¶

iduedu.get_gtfs_public_transport_graph(feed, *, service_date=None, service_period=None, service_dates=None, start_time=None, end_time=None, crs=None, single_departure_wait_min=None, walk_speed_m_per_min=83.33333333333333)[source]

Build a static public-transport UrbanGraph from a local GTFS feed.

Boarding weights use the first-iteration half-headway heuristic. Scheduled services use half the mean gap between consecutive departures of the same route pattern at the same stop. frequencies.txt uses headway_secs / 120.

A gap is a gap between departures of the same day. With service_date the graph is that day’s timetable. Without it each pattern-stop gets the average day’s wait: the wait of every service day of service_period – or of the feed’s own calendar, at most 366 days from its start – computed on that day’s trips alone, then averaged with every calendar day weighing the same, over the days the pattern-stop runs. Trips running on none of those days are left out. A feed without calendar/calendar_dates is taken as a single day.

start_time/end_time bound the departures within each day; None means unbounded.

Parameters:

  • feed (Union[str, Path, GTFSFeed, Sequence[str | Path | GTFSFeed]]) – Directory containing GTFS text files, a GTFS ZIP archive, an already-read iduedu.gtfs.GTFSFeed, or a sequence of any of those. A sequence is merged with iduedu.merge_gtfs_feeds() first, which qualifies identifiers by source; pass the merged feed yourself when you need to control prefixes or fuse stops across feeds.

  • service_date (date | datetime | str | None) – Optional service date used with calendar.txt and calendar_dates.txt. Accepts date, YYYY-MM-DD or YYYYMMDD.

  • service_period (tuple[date | datetime | str, date | datetime | str] | None) – Optional (first, last) dates, both inclusive, to average the waits over instead of the feed’s own calendar (for example a reference week). Cannot be combined with service_date.

  • service_dates (Optional[Sequence[date | datetime | str]]) – Optional explicit days to average the waits over, e.g. a reference week plus the own weeks of merged sources not in service then. Every day still holds only its own trips, so days of different years do not mix. Cannot be combined with service_date or service_period.

  • start_time (time | str | int | float | None) – Inclusive departure-time bound. None means unbounded.

  • end_time (time | str | int | float | None) – Exclusive departure-time bound. None means unbounded.

  • crs (Any | None) – Projected target CRS. If omitted, a local UTM CRS is estimated.

  • single_departure_wait_min (float | None) – Explicit wait fallback for a pattern-stop with one scheduled departure. If None, its boarding edge is omitted.

  • walk_speed_m_per_min (float) – Fallback speed for pathways without an explicit traversal time.

Return type:

UrbanGraph

iduedu.merge_gtfs_feeds(sources, *, prefixes=None, merge_stops_within=None, deduplicate=False, duplicate_platform_tolerance=0.5, crs=None, validate=True)[source]¶

Combine several GTFS feeds into one that the graph builder can consume.

Identifiers are qualified by their source, so a route_id of 1 in two feeds becomes two distinct routes. Definitions and references are rewritten together, and blank optional references stay blank.

Parameters:

  • sources (Sequence[str | Path | GTFSFeed]) – Paths to GTFS directories or ZIP archives, or already-read feeds. A single source is returned unchanged unless stop merging is requested, so this function is a no-op where merging is not needed.

  • prefixes (Optional[Sequence[str]]) – One prefix per source. Defaults to each source’s file name, with a numeric suffix where two sources share one.

  • merge_stops_within (float | None) – Distance in metres below which stops published by different feeds are treated as the same place and fused into one node. None (the default) keeps every stop distinct: fusing stops changes the topology of the network, and doing it silently would make an inferred transfer indistinguishable from a published one. Values around 15–25 m suit feeds from different agencies; the 1 m used for OSM platforms is far too tight here, because two agencies survey the same kerb independently.

  • deduplicate (bool) – Count once what several sources publish twice: fuse platforms of different sources within duplicate_platform_tolerance metres and drop trips another source already runs identically. See deduplicate_across_feeds(). Off by default: it changes the feed.

  • duplicate_platform_tolerance (float) – Metres within which two sources’ platforms are treated as one when deduplicate is on.

  • crs (Any | None) – Projected CRS in metres used for the distance test. Estimated from the stops when omitted.

  • validate (bool) – Run iduedu.gtfs.validate_gtfs_feed() on the result. Leave this on: it is what makes the merge verifiable rather than merely plausible.

Return type:

GTFSFeed

Returns:

A iduedu.gtfs.GTFSFeed whose source names the merged inputs.

Raises:

  • ValueError – If no sources are given, or the number of prefixes does not match the number of sources.

  • GTFSValidationError – If the merged feed breaks a GTFS contract.

iduedu.deduplicate_across_feeds(tables, prefixes, *, platform_tolerance=0.5, crs=None)[source]¶

Count once what several merged feeds publish twice. Edits tables in place.

Catalogues overlap: two versions of one feed, a national feed and a city feed of the same operator, a mirror and its original. Merged with qualified identifiers, the copies never collide – they sit side by side, and the graph gets two patterns where the city runs one. Two rules remove them, both narrow on purpose, because a wrong merge is worse than a leftover duplicate:

  • Platforms – stops rows with location_type 0 or empty, where passengers board; the graph builder makes them platform nodes, while the vehicle’s stopping place is a separate node per pattern and is not touched. Platforms of different sources within platform_tolerance metres are one platform. The default 0.5 m is measured: across 125 703 platforms of cities merged from several feeds, 63% had another source’s platform within 0.5 m (the same coordinate republished), while distinct platforms of one feed already occur 1-3 m apart. Stations, entrances, generic nodes and boarding areas are never fused, nor platforms of one source.

  • Trips – after that, a trip of one source is a copy of a trip of another when both run the same route_type through the same platforms with the same arrival and departure times (in seconds), pickup and drop-off rules and, for frequency-based trips, the same headways. The source that sorts first keeps it.

Parameters:

  • tables (dict[str, DataFrame]) – Merged tables keyed by GTFS table name without .txt, identifiers qualified as <prefix>:<id>.

  • prefixes (Sequence[str]) – The prefix of each source.

  • platform_tolerance (float) – Metres within which two sources’ platforms are one.

  • crs (Any | None) – Projected CRS in metres for the distance test; estimated when omitted.

Return type:

dict[str, int]

Returns:

{"platforms_fused": n, "trips_dropped": m}.
